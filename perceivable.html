a11yHub · WCAG 2.2 · Principle 1

Perceivable

If someone cannot see it, hear it or otherwise take it in, it does not exist for them. This principle has 29 criteria in four guidelines. Every level A and AA criterion below has a bench to try.

  • 1.2Time-based media

Guideline 1.1

Text Alternatives

Anything that is not text needs a text version, so it can be spoken, enlarged or turned into braille.

1.1.1
Level A

Non-text Content

Images, icons and other non-text content need a text alternative that does the same job. Pure decoration should be hidden from assistive technology.

Bench · shop with the screen off

Stoneware mug$38

A screen reader announces:

    Who it helps
    Blind and low-vision people, and anyone whose images fail to load.
    Fix
    Write alt that says what the image is for. Name icon buttons. Use alt="" for decoration.

    Guideline 1.2

    Time-based Media

    Audio and video carry information on two channels. Each channel needs a backup for people who cannot use it.

    1.2.1–5
    Level A and AAfive criteria, one bench

    Captions, audio description and transcripts

    Who it helps
    Deaf, hard-of-hearing, blind and deafblind people, plus anyone watching on mute on the train.
    Fix
    Caption every video. Script it so the speech names what is shown, or add a description track. Publish the transcript.

    Guideline 1.3

    Adaptable

    Content should survive being presented another way: spoken, linearised, rotated or restyled.

    1.3.1
    Level A

    Info and Relationships

    Structure you can see, such as headings, lists and field labels, has to exist in the code too. Looking like a heading is not enough.

    Bench · same look, different code

    What sighted visitors see (identical in both)

    Opening hours
    Weekdays 8 to 6
    Saturday 9 to 4
    Sunday closed
    Email for updates
    you@example.com

    What a screen reader user can navigate by

      Who it helps
      Screen reader users, who jump between headings, lists and fields the way sighted people skim.
      Fix
      Use <h2>, <ul>, <label for> and <th>. Style them however you like afterwards.
      1.3.2
      Level A

      Meaningful Sequence

      When order matters, the order in the code has to match the order on screen. Assistive technology reads the code.

      Bench · listen in code order

      1. Boil the kettle
      2. Add the tea bag
      3. Pour the water
      4. Wait three minutes

      On screen the recipe reads 1, 2, 3, 4. Press Read it aloud to hear the order in the code.

      Who it helps
      Screen reader and keyboard users, and people who turn off styles or use reader mode.
      Fix
      Write the HTML in reading order first. Be careful with CSS order, absolute positioning and layout tables.
      1.3.3
      Level A

      Sensory Characteristics

      Instructions cannot rely only on shape, colour, size, position or sound. “The round button on the right” means nothing to someone who cannot see the layout.

      Bench · follow the instruction

      BackHelpNext
      1. Back, button
      2. Help, button
      3. Next, button

      Who it helps
      Blind and colour-blind people, and anyone whose screen lays things out differently from yours.
      Fix
      Refer to controls by their label first. Add the visual cue as a bonus, never as the only clue.
      1.3.4
      Level AA

      Orientation

      Content must work in both portrait and landscape, unless one orientation is essential, as it is for a piano app.

      Bench · the phone is fixed to a wheelchair mount

      Boarding pass
      NZ 105
      Gate 12 · Seat 14C

      The phone is upright. Rotate it to landscape, the way it sits on a mount.

      Who it helps
      People whose device is mounted in one position, and people who need landscape for larger text.
      Fix
      Do not lock orientation in the app manifest or with CSS. Let the layout respond.
      1.3.5
      Level AA

      Identify Input Purpose

      Fields that ask for personal details must say what they are in code, so browsers and assistive tools can fill them in.

      Bench · fill in the form

      Press Use my saved details and see what the browser can do.

      Who it helps
      People with memory, dexterity or reading difficulties, for whom typing an address is slow or error-prone.
      Fix
      Add the standard tokens: autocomplete="name", "email", "tel", "postal-code".
      • 1.3.6Identify Purpose: icons, regions and controls expose their purpose in code so tools can swap in familiar symbols.AAA

      Guideline 1.4

      Distinguishable

      Make it easy to separate foreground from background, in sight and in sound.

      1.4.1
      Level A

      Use of Color

      Colour cannot be the only way to show meaning. Pair it with text, an icon or a pattern.

      Bench · which items are sold out?

      Who it helps
      About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a colour vision deficiency. Also anyone printing in black and white.
      Fix
      Add a label or icon to status colours. Underline links in body text. Mark form errors with text.
      1.4.2
      Level A

      Audio Control

      If sound plays by itself for more than 3 seconds, there must be a way to pause it, stop it or turn it down on the page.

      Bench · a screen reader against autoplay music

      “Sign in, heading level 1. Email, edit text. Password, edit text.”

      This demo makes no sound. The bars stand in for music that starts by itself.

      Who it helps
      Screen reader users, who cannot hear their software over the music, and people who are easily distracted.
      Fix
      Do not autoplay sound. If you must, put a pause control first on the page.
      1.4.3
      Level AA

      Contrast (Minimum)

      Text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background. Large text, 24px or 18.66px bold and up, needs 3:1.

      Bench · fade the text until it fails

      Large heading at 24px

      Body text at 16px. Drag the fade slider and watch how early this line becomes hard work.

      2.4:1from 1:1 (invisible) to 21:1 (black on white)
      3:14.5:17:1
      Body text, AA (4.5:1) Large text, AA (3:1) Body text, AAA (7:1, criterion 1.4.6)
      Who it helps
      People with low vision or colour vision deficiency, and anyone reading a phone outdoors.
      Fix
      Darken the text until the ratio reads 4.5:1 or more. Logos and disabled controls are exempt.
      1.4.4
      Level AA

      Resize Text

      People must be able to enlarge text to 200% without losing content or function.

      Bench · turn the text size up

      Your bookingFlight NZ 105 to Sydney leaves at 7:45 am from gate 12.Change seat
      Who it helps
      People with low vision who do not use a magnifier, including many older readers.
      Fix
      Size text in rem, let containers grow, and never set a fixed height on something that holds text.
      1.4.5
      Level AA

      Images of Text

      Use real text, not a picture of text, unless the exact look is essential, as with a logo.

      Bench · zoom in on a banner

      Text saved as an image

      Real text

      Summer sale: 20% off everything

      The image blurs as it grows and ignores colour preferences. The real text stays sharp and adapts.

      Who it helps
      People who zoom, change fonts or colours, or use a screen reader or translation tool.
      Fix
      Set headlines and banners as HTML text styled with CSS. Keep images of text for logos.
      1.4.10
      Level AA

      Reflow

      At 320 CSS pixels wide, which is a desktop page zoomed to 400%, content must fit without scrolling in two directions.

      Bench · zoom a desktop page to 400%

      Menu
      Home
      Fares
      Help
      Timetable changes

      From Monday the 8:10 service to the city leaves five minutes earlier and no longer stops at Harbour Road. Check your connection before you travel.

      Who it helps
      People with low vision who zoom heavily. Scrolling sideways on every line makes reading exhausting.
      Fix
      Use flexible grids, max-width and media queries. Tables, maps and diagrams are exempt.
      1.4.11
      Level AA

      Non-text Contrast

      The parts of controls and graphics you need to see, such as input borders, icons and chart lines, need 3:1 contrast against what is next to them.

      Bench · find the form field

      Search
      1.2:1
      Who it helps
      People with low vision, who otherwise cannot tell where a field is or what state a toggle is in.
      Fix
      Give borders, icons, focus rings and chart marks a colour of at least 3:1. On white, #949494 just passes.
      1.4.12
      Level AA

      Text Spacing

      Nothing may break when a reader sets line height to 1.5, paragraph spacing to 2, letter spacing to 0.12 and word spacing to 0.16 times the font size.

      Bench · apply a reader’s spacing

      Your parcel is out for delivery and should arrive before noon.

      Someone must be home to sign for it. Reply HOLD to collect it instead.

      Who it helps
      People with dyslexia or low vision, many of whom read far better with looser text.
      Fix
      Let text containers grow. Avoid fixed heights and overflow: hidden around copy.
      1.4.13
      Level AA

      Content on Hover or Focus

      Tooltips and popovers that appear on hover or focus must be dismissible, hoverable and persistent.

      Bench · read the password rules

      Password
      At least 12 characters, one number, and no part of your name or email address.
      Confirm password
      Create account
      Dismissible: you can close it without moving away Hoverable: the pointer can move onto it Persistent: it stays until you are done

      Press Rules. The flaky tooltip covers the fields below and vanishes before you can finish reading.

      Who it helps
      People using screen magnifiers, who must move the pointer across the tooltip to read it, and slower readers.
      Fix
      Close on Esc, keep it open while the pointer is over it, and never hide it on a timer.

      • 1.4.7Low or No Background Audio: speech recordings have background sound at least 20 dB quieter, or none.AAA
      • 1.4.8Visual Presentation: readers can pick colours, lines stay under 80 characters, no justified text, generous spacing.AAA
      • 1.4.9Images of Text (No Exception): only for pure decoration or where essential.AAA
